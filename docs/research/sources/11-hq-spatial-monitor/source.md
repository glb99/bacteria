## Why it was cited

Brought by the human, not by `idea.md`. They had read it the day before and asked
whether a representation of this kind would suit the shared mental model this
project is building — specifically as a way to make the ontology legible between
a person and their agent, and to reduce the cognitive cost of reading it.

The relevance is not Mars. It is that HQ takes a body of data nobody reads in
tabular form and gives it a **place**, on a stated rule that every built volume
corresponds to one real record and nothing is invented to fill a gap — which is a
rendering discipline for a graph, and the closest thing any source so far offers
to §8's negotiation surface as an *interface* rather than as a route.

## Completeness

The page is a scrolling case study around an interactive 3D scene. The prose is
captured in full: header, brief, all seven "Sol" entries, the three "under the
hood" notes, and the footer.

**Not captured**: the 3D colony itself, the flyover, the inventory page linked at
Sol 014, and the screenshots each Sol entry captions. `WebFetch` returned a
summary rather than the text, so the raw file was extracted from the fetched HTML
directly; anything rendered client-side by the scene is absent by construction.

The argument of the piece survives without them — it is made in the prose, and the
Sol entries state each rule explicitly — but any claim here about how the *scene*
actually reads is an inference from description, not observation.
